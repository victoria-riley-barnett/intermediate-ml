A sequence of forty-eight tokens becomes a grid of forty-eight rows, one vector per position. Earlier chapters produced that grid and then collapsed it, by <Ref to="averaging-rows">averaging</Ref>, by <Ref to="flattening">flattening</Ref>, or by <Ref to="convolution">sliding a window</Ref>, and each collapse threw away a relation between two positions far apart. The grid itself was never the problem. Nothing so far has let one row consult another, and attention is that operation.

<Page name="attention-as-averaging" />

The rest of the chapter is about where the shares come from: first from the rows as they are, then from learned projections of them.
